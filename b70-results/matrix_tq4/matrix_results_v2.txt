## Analysis

1. XPU Graph decode speedup is most pronounced at LOW concurrency:
   - c=1 64K: 3.0x per-request decode rate, 1.81x aggregate
   - c=1 128K: 1.93x per-request decode rate, 1.27x aggregate
   At low concurrency, decode is 1 token at a time -- graph overhead dominates.
   At high concurrency (c=8), decode batches 8 tokens -- overhead is amortized.

2. TTFT is unchanged (expected): Prefill always runs eagerly (not graphed).
   Graph only accelerates the decode phase.

3. Aggregate improvement diminishes with concurrency because:
   - Prefill dominates wall time at high concurrency (serial prefill queuing)
   - Decode batch size is larger, so per-step overhead fraction shrinks
   - The 256 output tokens are a small fraction of total compute

4. All 64K/128K cells now work with XPU Graph (previously crashed).
   The workspace fix pre-reserves 256MB at init for max_model_len=131072,
   so the TQ attention kernel always has enough workspace regardless of
   when graph capture vs. inference happens.

## Updated Key Findings (v2)

1. Workspace fix eliminates the TQ4+Graph 64K+ crash. ALL cells now run
   with XPU Graph decode, making TQ4 vs FP8 comparisons fair.

2. TQ4 now BEATS FP8 at 64K across all concurrency levels:
   - c=1: 14.7 vs 14.4 (+2%)
   - c=2: 16.4 vs 15.0 (+9%)
   - c=4: 17.6 vs 14.8 (+19%)
   - c=8: 18.3 vs N/A (FP8 cannot serve c=8 at 64K)

3. TQ4 dominates at 128K:
   - c=1: 6.6 vs 5.1 (+29%)
   - c=2: 7.0 vs 4.8 (+46%)
   - c=4: 7.1 vs N/A (FP8 cannot serve c=4 at 128K)

4. The workspace fix + XPU Graph gives TQ4 the best of both worlds:
   - 2.53x KV budget vs FP8 (long context capacity)
   - Competitive or better throughput at 64K+ (graph decode)
   - Slightly lower throughput at short context (TQ dequant overhead)

5. Regression check passed: c=1 4K = 64.0 tok/s (was 63.2), no degradation.
